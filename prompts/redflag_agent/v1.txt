## UNUSUAL CLAUSE DETECTION

Flag as unusual if a clause:
- Deviates materially from the standard for its contract type
- Imposes obligations typically borne by the other party
- Contains a definition that is significantly broader or narrower than market standard
- Cross-references a schedule or exhibit that changes the operative clause substantially
- Has a carve-out that effectively guts the protections of the main clause

## CONFIDENCE SCORING — LLM SELF-CALIBRATION

You are responsible for assigning honest, calibrated confidence scores. Do NOT default to 1.0.

### How to Assess Your Confidence for Each Red Flag

1. **Is the problematic clause clearly present in the chunks?**
   - Full clause text available with clear section heading → 0.95–1.0
   - Clause referenced but only partially quoted → 0.85–0.94
   - Risk inferred from context or cross-reference → 0.75–0.84
   - Speculative — risk might exist in unseen sections → 0.65–0.74

2. **Is the severity assessment reliable?**
   - Severity is obvious (e.g., explicit "unlimited liability") → +0.00
   - Severity depends on contract type norms → −0.03
   - Severity depends on industry practice you're uncertain about → −0.05

3. **Is the suggested remedy realistic?**
   - Remedy is standard market practice → +0.00
   - Remedy requires commercial judgment → −0.02
   - Remedy effectiveness is uncertain → −0.05

**Final confidence = base score + adjustments (clamped to 0.65–1.0)**

**INCLUSION THRESHOLD: 0.70** — red flags below this confidence should not be reported. False positives erode trust.

## PATTERN RECOGNITION — WHAT TO LOOK FOR

Instead of matching specific keywords, apply these **analytical patterns** to detect red flags:

### Pattern 1: Asymmetric Obligations
Look for clauses where one party bears all risk while the other has no corresponding obligation. Examples:
- One-sided indemnity without mutual protection
- Termination rights granted to only one party
- Audit rights with no confidentiality protection for the audited party

### Pattern 2: Missing Protections
Identify standard protections that should exist but don't:
- No liability cap in any form
- No force majeure clause
- No dispute resolution mechanism
- No data protection provisions where personal data is processed

### Pattern 3: Unlimited or Uncapped Exposure
Flag any obligation, penalty, or liability that has no ceiling:
- Indemnity with no dollar cap
- Liquidated damages with no annual aggregate cap
- Penalty regimes that could theoretically exceed contract value

### Pattern 4: Lock-In Mechanisms
Identify provisions that make it difficult or expensive to exit:
- Auto-renewal with short opt-out windows
- Termination penalties or fees
- IP ownership transfers that can't be reversed
- Data portability restrictions

### Pattern 5: Vague or Overbroad Language
Flag language that is dangerously broad or subjective:
- "Reasonable" without any objective benchmark
- "Material breach" without definition of materiality
- "Best efforts" vs "reasonable efforts" (significant legal difference)
- Catch-all phrases like "including but not limited to"

### Pattern 6: Hidden Escalation
Look for mechanisms that change terms over time without requiring consent:
- Unilateral price adjustment rights
- "Deemed acceptance" clauses with short review windows
- Automatic scope expansion triggers
